# ARCnet Economic Constitution

## Status and Authority

This document is a **working constitutional draft** assembled during Wave XXIV — Repository Canonicalization.

Sections I–IV record decisions ratified by the Human Architect during the Wave XXIV constitutional design process. They extend the existing canonical economic posture in `../governance/economic-principles.md`, `../governance/treasury-constitution.md`, and `../governance/governance-specification.md` without silently replacing those documents while this file remains a working draft.

Unresolved numerical parameters are intentionally left open. No number, percentage, emission rate, reward rate, exchange price, vesting schedule, or reserve-release schedule should be inferred from this document unless explicitly ratified later.

The design process for later sections is:

1. ground the question in existing repository canon;
2. identify the unresolved constitutional choice;
3. develop a coherent proposal and alternatives;
4. obtain explicit Human Architect approval, rejection, or revision;
5. only then incorporate the decision into this working draft and, when appropriate, promote it to canon.

# Section I — Purpose of the Economy

## 1.1 Purpose

The ARCnet economy exists to create a durable, participant-owned economic substrate through which useful participation, human care, creation, stewardship, contribution, collective discovery, and shared prosperity can generate and circulate real value.

Its purpose is not merely to monetize an application or sustain a token. It is to support an ecosystem in which participants can create, exchange, preserve, and steward value without surrendering identity sovereignty, human dignity, or temporal integrity.

The economy should be capable of supporting both present human needs and long-horizon civilizational work. Legitimate long-term missions may include scientific discovery, ecological restoration, education, infrastructure, health, cultural creation, space exploration, orbital infrastructure, resource development, and future construction beyond Earth. No single mission is a compulsory ideology for ARCnet participants.

## 1.2 Recognition Without Ranking Humanity

Economic systems may recognize useful contribution, verified service, stewardship, creation, or other bounded forms of participation.

They may not convert those observations into a universal measure of human worth.

**Constitutional principle:**

> The economy may recognize contribution. It may not rank humanity.

# Section II — Nature of MANA

## 2.1 Unified Primitive

MANA is ARCnet's unified transferable utility-value primitive.

It has two inseparable economic aspects:

- **capacity** — the ability to authorize or consume bounded optional utilities and infrastructure resources;
- **transferable value** — the ability to exchange value between participants, applications, builders, service providers, and governed ecosystem mechanisms.

ARCnet does not constitutionally require separate currencies for utility and transferable economic value.

## 2.2 Permitted Functions

Subject to constitutional and protocol bounds, MANA may coordinate:

- optional application capacity;
- infrastructure usage;
- exchange and commerce;
- creator and builder support;
- security services;
- protocol operations;
- proposal deposits and bounded governance participation;
- governed treasury routing;
- other explicitly authorized utility surfaces.

## 2.3 Forbidden Purchases

MANA may not purchase or fabricate:

- Vitae recognition;
- ACC / ChainCode continuity or identity legitimacy;
- elapsed time or accelerated Tempus progression;
- fabricated participation history;
- human worth;
- governance supremacy;
- exemption from constitutional invariants.

Payment may expand capacity. It may not accelerate becoming.

## 2.4 Optional Non-Transferable Participation Primitive

ARCnet may later adopt a separate non-transferable, identity-bound participation primitive if it improves accessibility, anti-abuse design, or issuance control.

Such a primitive would not itself be MANA and must not become a hidden reputation score, transferable claim on human worth, or substitute for governance legitimacy.

Its adoption remains unresolved and requires separate constitutional design.

# Section III — Constitutional Economic Invariants

The following laws bind all ARCnet economic mechanisms, applications, governance actions, treasury operations, issuance programs, and future parameter regimes unless explicitly superseded by a valid constitutional amendment that preserves the deeper dignity and sovereignty constraints of the system.

## 3.1 No Permanent Debt Dependency

ARCnet and MANA must not require perpetual debt expansion for basic monetary or protocol viability.

Credit arrangements may exist at application or participant layers where lawfully and explicitly designed, but the protocol economy must not structurally depend on ever-expanding debt to preserve monetary function.

## 3.2 No Acceleration of Becoming Through Payment

MANA may purchase capacity, optional services, infrastructure usage, or other legitimate utility.

It may not compress or bypass time-based progression, Tempus windows, Vitae thresholds, identity age, or other mechanisms intended to represent lived continuity.

## 3.3 No Authority for Sale

Wealth may participate in governance only as one bounded factor within a multi-factor authority system.

MANA ownership alone may never purchase governing supremacy, constitutional exemption, identity legitimacy, or unilateral protocol control.

## 3.4 No Human-Worth Scoring

ARCnet may maintain narrow factual metrics necessary for security, eligibility, service verification, accounting, or governance.

It may not aggregate participant behavior into a universal social-credit score, human-value score, or coercive rank that determines a person's worth.

## 3.5 Explicit Pricing and Legible Receipts

Economic consequences must be visible before authorization.

MANA charges, deposits, burns, routing, recurring commitments, and other material economic effects must be legible to the participant before confirmation and reconstructable afterward through appropriate receipts or audit records.

Hidden fees and intentionally obscure economic routing are prohibited.

## 3.6 Utility-Bound Sinks and No Coercive Extraction

MANA sinks must correspond to real utility, infrastructure consumption, risk, security, coordination, legitimate anti-abuse requirements, or explicitly authorized optional services.

Sinks may not be designed primarily to exploit psychological pressure, punish silence, manufacture compulsive participation, or force monetization unrelated to real system cost or value.

## 3.7 Sovereign Custody and Affirmative Consent

Participant-controlled custody is the default economic posture.

Transfers, spending permissions, delegations, recurring authorizations, and custody changes must be affirmative, explicit, and appropriately scoped.

No economic mechanism may silently convert participation into an open-ended authorization over participant assets.

## 3.8 Bounded and Transparent Issuance

No hidden, discretionary, or unilateral minting is permitted.

All MANA issuance must arise from constitutionally authorized mechanisms operating under visible quantitative bounds, publicly auditable rules, and defined governance authority.

## 3.9 Basic Participation Remains Accessible

Basic ARCnet participation must not require MANA merely to exist within the network.

At minimum, the economic system must preserve non-paywalled access to identity continuity, basic Hope functionality, participant custody access, personal economic receipts, and ordinary baseline participation appropriate to the active protocol phase.

Optional capacity and resource-intensive services may be priced. Human continuity may not be.

## 3.10 Treasury Stewardship, Not Ownership

Treasury resources are shared ecosystem resources held in stewardship under constitutional rules.

No custodian, Architect, validator, governance participant, committee, or implementation team owns Treasury resources by virtue of role.

Treasury actions require bounded authorization, public auditability, and compliance with the Treasury Constitution.

# Section IV — MANA Issuance

## 4.0 Issuance Boundary

The constitution distinguishes creation of new MANA from movement or reuse of existing MANA.

- **Transfer is not issuance.**
- **Treasury spending is not issuance.**
- **Recycling existing MANA is not issuance.**
- **Minting new supply is issuance.**

Only constitutionally authorized monetary mechanisms may create new MANA.

## 4.1 Genesis Issuance

Genesis Issuance creates the initial MANA supply.

Genesis is:

- singular;
- publicly auditable;
- constitutionally authorized;
- separated from personal human authority and identity;
- not a Founder or Architect reward.

The constitutional Genesis allocation categories are:

1. **Treasury Foundation** — establishes governed shared-resource capacity;
2. **Protocol Reserve** — bootstraps security, infrastructure, and network continuity;
3. **Ecosystem Development** — supports builders, research, tooling, applications, public goods, and ecosystem formation;
4. **Community Genesis Distribution** — enables broad participant access and initial circulation under an explicitly defined distribution program;
5. **Strategic Reserve** — preserves bounded long-horizon capacity for constitutionally legitimate future needs.

No personal Founder/Architect Genesis allocation is created by title or founding status.

The Human Architect and other contributors may be compensated for actual development, stewardship, representation, operations, or other legitimate work through transparent mechanisms that are available under the same constitutional standards applied to other contributors.

Genesis supply size, allocation percentages, circulation schedule, reserve release, and distribution mechanics remain unresolved pending modeling.

## 4.2 Monetary Supply Policy

MANA follows a **constitutionally bounded adaptive supply** model.

Genesis establishes the initial supply. Later issuance is permitted only when:

- the purpose is constitutionally authorized;
- the issuance mechanism is explicitly defined;
- the action remains within hard quantitative ceilings;
- governance requirements are satisfied where applicable;
- the resulting issuance is publicly auditable.

Inflation is not mandatory.

Authorized issuance may decrease, pause, or reach zero when system conditions do not justify new supply. Constitutionally authorized burns or other sinks may offset issuance.

No governance body may treat continued inflation as an entitlement.

## 4.3 Protocol Issuance

Protocol Issuance may compensate verified infrastructure and security services necessary to operate ARCnet, including where applicable:

- consensus security;
- durable storage;
- computation;
- relay and network service;
- protocol maintenance or other verified infrastructure functions.

### 4.3.1 Reserve-First Funding

The Protocol Reserve established at Genesis, together with protocol revenue and recycled existing MANA, should fund early network incentives before reliance on new issuance where practical.

New Protocol Issuance is a bounded monetary mechanism, not the default source of every infrastructure payment.

### 4.3.2 Nodes and Consensus Validators

Ordinary ARCnet-capable phones or participant devices may function as nodes without automatically becoming consensus validators.

Consensus validation is a distinct security role with explicit technical, economic, and governance requirements.

### 4.3.3 Reward Semantics

Validator and security compensation should operate through bounded epoch, security, or service budgets rather than an assumption that every transaction mechanically creates a new reward.

Infrastructure compensation is payment for verified service. It is not guaranteed yield.

Specific validator, storage, compute, relay, and maintenance reward formulas remain unresolved pending protocol and economic modeling.

## 4.4 Treasury Issuance

Treasury Issuance may exist as a **secondary monetary instrument** for constitutionally authorized shared needs.

The Treasury does not possess independent mint authority.

The required authority flow is:

**Governance authorization → constitutional issuance validation → ARCnet monetary module → named Treasury allocation lane.**

Before Treasury Issuance is used, governance must consider available Treasury balances, revenues, reserves, and other non-inflationary resources.

Treasury Issuance requires an elevated governance threshold appropriate to monetary expansion and must remain within an independent hard ceiling.

Treasury Issuance may not be used merely to:

- repair ordinary mismanagement;
- conceal insolvency or budget failure;
- manipulate the market price of MANA;
- enrich insiders;
- bypass Treasury allocation or custody controls;
- evade constitutional issuance bounds.

Exact thresholds and ceilings remain unresolved.

## 4.5 Ecosystem Development Issuance

New MANA may temporarily support ecosystem development that produces defined shared value when existing development reserves and Treasury resources are insufficient or intentionally preserved under an approved policy.

Eligible purposes may include:

- protocol and application software;
- developer tooling;
- security work;
- research;
- documentation;
- accessibility;
- scientific work;
- public goods;
- shared infrastructure;
- other governance-approved development with demonstrable ecosystem value.

Existing Genesis and Treasury development resources should be used before new development issuance where practical.

Every development issuance program must be:

- finite in duration or budget;
- bounded by an explicit ceiling;
- tied to defined work, deliverables, milestones, or verified contribution;
- auditable;
- subject to conflict-of-interest disclosure;
- designed so reliance on issuance can decrease as the ecosystem becomes more self-funding.

Compensation follows actual work and responsibility, not Founder, Architect, validator, or governance title alone.

Publicly funded development must disclose the applicable intellectual-property and licensing posture so the ecosystem can understand what shared value it is funding.

## 4.6 Emergency Constitutional Issuance

Emergency Constitutional Issuance is the narrowest and most exceptional MANA issuance mechanism.

It may be considered only in response to a severe systemic threat to ARCnet continuity, security, or recoverability, including circumstances such as:

- catastrophic protocol exploits;
- consensus collapse;
- emergency cryptographic migration;
- loss or corruption of critical network state;
- catastrophic custody or settlement failure;
- another existential continuity event of comparable severity.

Normal reserves, Treasury resources, protocol revenue, insurance or recovery mechanisms where applicable, and other non-inflationary remedies must be considered first.

Emergency Issuance requires:

- the highest monetary governance threshold defined by the constitutional system;
- an independent hard issuance ceiling;
- explicit public incident justification;
- defined recipient or allocation lanes;
- mandatory auditability;
- automatic sunset of extraordinary authority;
- mandatory post-event constitutional review.

No individual, including the Human Architect, may invoke Emergency Issuance alone.

Emergency Issuance may not be used to:

- defend or target MANA's market price;
- reimburse ordinary speculative losses;
- rescue private actors from normal market risk;
- bypass ordinary budgeting because it is politically inconvenient;
- create standing emergency mint authority.

The emergency ceiling and ratification mechanics remain unresolved pending later governance and monetary design.

## 4.7 Unresolved Monetary Parameters

The following are deliberately **not yet constitutionalized**:

- total Genesis supply;
- Genesis allocation percentages;
- initial circulating supply;
- Community Genesis Distribution amount and mechanics;
- initial exchange price or price-discovery posture;
- annual or epoch issuance ceilings;
- validator reward rates;
- storage reward rates;
- compute reward rates;
- relay or other infrastructure reward rates;
- Treasury Issuance ceilings;
- Ecosystem Development Issuance ceilings;
- Emergency Constitutional Issuance ceilings;
- vesting schedules;
- reserve-release schedules;
- burn percentages and fee-routing percentages.

These parameters require explicit modeling, simulation, risk analysis, and Human Architect ratification before they become constitutional or operational policy.
