# Arcanum Experience & Civilizational Roadmap

## Purpose

This roadmap defines the human-experience, symbolic, curriculum, capability, and civilizational development spine that must advance alongside ARCnet substrate construction.

It does not grant implementation authority by itself. It distinguishes canonical constraints, implemented state, implementation candidates, planned milestones, visionary hypotheses, and unresolved questions so that future work can proceed without confusing infrastructure with embodiment.

## Core finding — the embodiment gap

Arcanum does not presently suffer primarily from an absence of content. It has an **embodiment gap**.

The repository already contains constitutional doctrine, canonical modules, deep Vitae curriculum architecture, specialization systems, anatomy and lexicon work, substantial Guardian content, Hope, Tempus, Nexus, Wallet, Architect tooling, and ARCnet foundations.

What remains comparatively underdeveloped is the ability for a participant to **see, navigate, inhabit, connect, practice, and understand those relationships as one coherent world**.

## Status discipline

Roadmap language must preserve the following distinctions:

- **canonical** — ratified authority or binding doctrine;
- **implemented** — present in a verified repository or deployed surface;
- **implementation-candidate** — approved branch-level or design-level material awaiting promotion or implementation evidence;
- **planned** — approved direction without implementation;
- **visionary** — long-range hypothesis or design horizon;
- **unresolved** — intentionally open and requiring explicit evidence or Human Architect decision.

No symbolic, curricular, economic, governance, or civilizational proposal becomes canonical merely because it appears in this roadmap.

## Symbolic design grammar

### Hope — Seed of Life

**Status: implementation-candidate design grammar.**

Seed-of-Life geometry may organize presence, reflection, relation, emergence, and generative interior experience within Hope.

### Vitae — Tree of Life

**Status: repository-grounded symbolic and curriculum architecture; exact mappings require canon evidence.**

The Vitae corpus already uses Tree-of-Life and planetary organization. The Tree may organize spheres, paths, curriculum, transformation, stabilization, responsibility, and cross-domain knowledge.

Do **not** assert that each of the ten Vitae grades maps one-to-one to a Sephirah unless the curriculum canon explicitly establishes that mapping.

### Whole system — Fruit of Life

**Status: visionary / implementation-candidate hypothesis.**

The mature system may use Fruit-of-Life geometry with the **sovereign human at the experiential center**.

The current system has ten canonical modules. A possible future twelve-module topology must **not** be created merely to satisfy geometry.

Before adding any module, ask:

> What first-class capability required by the mature system cannot legitimately live inside an existing canonical module?

Only a real capability gap may justify expansion.

## Epistemic integrity

Arcanum may preserve multiple modes of human inquiry without flattening them into one truth category.

### Empirical science

Examples include anatomy, physiology, biology, chemistry, physics, botany, ecology, astronomy, and material science.

### Historical / traditional knowledge

Examples include historical alchemy, herbal traditions, sacred geometry traditions, planetary symbolism, and occult-anatomy traditions.

Historical existence does not establish every traditional claim as empirically true.

### Symbolic / interpretive practice

Examples include archetypes, correspondences, rites, mythic personification, and contemplative symbolism.

### Subjective / exploratory consciousness

Examples may include meditation, lucid dreaming, altered-state practices, remote-viewing claims, astral-projection claims, and other consciousness exploration.

Arcanum may support responsible inquiry without representing unresolved claims as established empirical fact.

## Participant capability

The key civilizational question is not merely:

> How many users exist?

It is:

> What can this population actually understand, create, maintain, repair, research, teach, or steward?

Potential capability domains include plant and ecological literacy, chemistry and material literacy, fabrication, repair, astronomy, computing, software development, scientific method, food cultivation and preparation, teaching, research, community organization, and infrastructure stewardship.

Vitae can recognize stabilized capability and gate responsibilities affecting others without converting capability into human worth.

## Vitae / MANA boundary

Vitae recognition is **economically sterile**.

MANA may never:

- purchase recognition;
- accelerate readiness;
- bypass a Vitae threshold;
- purchase human worth or status.

Actions around curriculum may independently produce factual receipts under other modules, and separate economy policy may reward eligible participation, contribution, builder work, or infrastructure contribution.

The governing distinction is:

> **Contribution may have economic consequences. Becoming does not have a price.**

Vitae itself does not mint value.

## Civilizational meaning of shared storage and computation

Shared infrastructure should follow actual participant activity.

If participants increasingly perform ecological observation, scientific modeling, plant recognition, astronomical analysis, material simulation, AI inference, research, knowledge indexing, rendering, and application hosting, then shared computation becomes the collective compute requirement generated by a civilization actively learning and building.

Shared storage may hold voluntarily contributed or public scientific datasets, species imagery, ecological observations, educational media, research outputs, public archives, application packages, models, and cultural works.

Infrastructure follows human capability rather than existing as an abstract decentralization feature.
