# ARCnet Inner Junction Neighbor-Edge Audit

## Purpose

The six current inner junctions already survived individual composition studies:

```text
J1 Commons Witness
J2 Practice Safety
J3 Cultural Commons
J4 Market Assurance
J5 Creator Public Goods
J6 Standards and Rights
```

This study asks a different question:

> **When two neighboring junctions of the inner octahedron must coordinate, does their relationship expose a distinct system function without creating a new sovereign layer?**

The study treats the inner octahedron as a derived coordination geometry.

It does not create junction-owned databases, constitutional authority, chain authority, or mandatory runtime services.

Candidate law remains controlling:

> **An inner junction is a composed coordination function where a generative relationship encounters a stewardship responsibility; it does not own either relationship and does not create new authority.**

## Fixed antipodal pairs

The three antipodal junction axes are:

```text
J1 Commons Witness
↔
J6 Standards and Rights

J2 Practice Safety
↔
J5 Creator Public Goods

J3 Cultural Commons
↔
J4 Market Assurance
```

Antipodes are deep system tensions, not direct octahedral edges.

Therefore each junction has four neighboring junctions, producing twelve undirected neighbor relations.

## Twelve junction-neighbor relationships

```text
01 J1 Commons Witness ↔ J2 Practice Safety
02 J1 Commons Witness ↔ J3 Cultural Commons
03 J1 Commons Witness ↔ J4 Market Assurance
04 J1 Commons Witness ↔ J5 Creator Public Goods

05 J6 Standards and Rights ↔ J2 Practice Safety
06 J6 Standards and Rights ↔ J3 Cultural Commons

09 J2 Practice Safety ↔ J3 Cultural Commons
10 J2 Practice Safety ↔ J4 Market Assurance
11 J5 Creator Public Goods ↔ J3 Cultural Commons
12 J5 Creator Public Goods ↔ J4 Market Assurance
```

The audit asks whether each produces a real coordination problem already implied by existing domains and laws.

---

# 01 — Commons Witness ↔ Practice Safety

Core coordination:

```text
publicly understandable evidence
↔
physical / practical risk response
```

Scenario:

A participant reports or discusses a public-resource-supported practice whose real-world safety claim is disputed.

Required separation:

```text
witness artifact
≠
risk determination
≠
policy action
```

Useful flow:

```text
bounded witness evidence
→ claim typing
→ safety review if warranted
→ separate authorized response
```

Finding:

> **Witness–Intervention Separation — public visibility of concern may trigger review, but concern itself does not authorize protective or governance action.**

Risk if collapsed:

- outrage becomes policy;
- anecdote becomes universal hazard evidence;
- safety authority suppresses lawful discussion.

Result: **strong**.

---

# 02 — Commons Witness ↔ Cultural Commons

Core coordination:

```text
public-resource evidence / memory
↔
cultural support / expression
```

Scenario:

A publicly supported cultural work or program is discussed, challenged, archived, or interpreted by participants.

Required separation:

```text
public funding evidence
≠
cultural worth
≠
creator identity
```

Useful flow:

```text
support/provenance facts
→ human-readable witness
→ plural cultural interpretation
```

Finding:

> **Public Support Must Remain Publicly Witnessable Without Making Cultural Meaning Publicly Ownable.**

Risk if collapsed:

- funding becomes ownership of meaning;
- public reporting becomes cultural ranking;
- controversy becomes automatic funding revocation.

Result: **strong**.

---

# 03 — Commons Witness ↔ Market Assurance

Core coordination:

```text
publicly understandable evidence
↔
technical / transactional assurance
```

Scenario:

A participant sees an assurance statement, provenance record, audit result, or market claim and needs to understand what was actually verified.

Useful flow:

```text
technical assurance artifact
→ bounded human-readable witness
→ exact scope explanation
```

Finding:

> **Assurance Must Be Explainable Without Expanding Its Scope — translating verification into human language may not convert object-specific assurance into generalized trust.**

Risk if collapsed:

- verified package → trusted person;
- verified transaction → moral legitimacy;
- public explanation overstates technical evidence.

Result: **very strong**.

---

# 04 — Commons Witness ↔ Creator Public Goods

Core coordination:

```text
community-observed need / evidence
↔
shared infrastructure support
```

Scenario:

Participants repeatedly encounter a missing creative/public capability and produce evidence of the need.

Useful flow:

```text
community observations
→ bounded need synthesis
→ public-goods proposal
→ separate design/funding review
```

Finding:

> **Witness Can Inform Public-Goods Need Without Becoming Funding Authority.**

Risk if collapsed:

- popularity creates budget entitlement;
- volume of complaints becomes automatic priority;
- social visibility displaces constitutional allocation process.

Result: **very strong**.

---

# 05 — Standards and Rights ↔ Practice Safety

Core coordination:

```text
durable rule / right / interoperability scope
↔
concrete safety response
```

Scenario:

A safety issue may require a standard, warning rule, product requirement, or bounded governance response.

Useful flow:

```text
specific hazard evidence
→ claim type
→ existing standard applicability
→ proposed standard change if necessary
→ governance only where scope requires
```

Finding:

> **Safety Exception Does Not Silently Become General Standard — urgent protective action and durable rulemaking are separate authority paths.**

Risk if collapsed:

- emergency response becomes permanent regulation;
- one incident rewrites broad standards;
- standards suppress private symbolic practice beyond actual hazard.

Result: **very strong**.

---

# 06 — Standards and Rights ↔ Cultural Commons

Core coordination:

```text
rights / licensing / interoperability
↔
cultural support / expression
```

Scenario:

A cultural public-goods program must respect creator rights, licensing, attribution, interoperability, and voluntary participation.

Useful flow:

```text
cultural proposal
→ rights/licensing declaration
→ support terms
→ creator-controlled publication/use boundaries
```

Finding:

> **Patronage Terms Must Declare Rights Scope Before Support — funding cannot silently acquire authorship, identity, meaning, or perpetual control.**

Risk if collapsed:

- grants become assignment of rights;
- public support creates default public-domain assumptions;
- interoperability standards override creator choice.

Result: **very strong**.

# 07 — Standards and Rights ↔ Market Assurance

Core coordination:

```text
standard / rights semantics
↔
verification / provenance / transaction assurance
```

Scenario:

A marketplace claims an object complies with a technical, ownership, licensing, custody, or transfer standard.

Useful flow:

```text
named standard
→ exact assurance target
→ evidence
→ bounded verification claim
```

Finding:

> **Compliance Is Typed — an assurance must name the standard, version, object, scope, and evidence rather than emitting generalized trust.**

Risk if collapsed:

- standard compliance → quality score;
- license validity → creator legitimacy;
- protocol compliance → governance endorsement.

Result: **very strong**.

# 08 — Standards and Rights ↔ Creator Public Goods

Core coordination:

```text
shared standards / rights
↔
publicly supported infrastructure
```

Scenario:

Publicly funded infrastructure needs APIs, file formats, portability, licensing, maintenance, or accessibility commitments.

Useful flow:

```text
public-goods proposal
→ interoperability/rights requirements
→ design
→ funding
→ versioned maintenance obligations
```

Finding:

> **Publicly Supported Infrastructure Should Declare Portability and Exit Rights Before Dependency Forms.**

Risk if collapsed:

- public funding creates permanent vendor lock-in;
- standards are chosen to advantage insiders;
- maintenance duty becomes indefinite without governance review.

Result: **very strong**.

---

# 09 — Practice Safety ↔ Cultural Commons

Core coordination:

```text
physical/practical safety
↔
cultural/symbolic expression
```

Scenario:

A cultural or symbolic practice has a physical component that may create actual risk.

This is one of the most sensitive junction-neighbor relations because the system must distinguish:

```text
symbolic meaning
from
physical claim
from
physical action
from
measurable hazard
```

Useful flow:

```text
cultural expression
→ identify physical action/claim if any
→ safety review only of that bounded action/claim
→ preserve symbolic meaning outside safety authority
```

Finding:

> **Safety May Govern Harmful Action Without Governing Symbolic Meaning as Such.**

This reinforces dignity and pluralism doctrine.

Risk if collapsed:

- beliefs classified as hazards;
- cultural dissent suppressed under safety language;
- symbolic statement treated as physical claim.

Result: **critical and strong**.

# 10 — Practice Safety ↔ Market Assurance

Core coordination:

```text
real-world hazard / product claim
↔
technical or transactional verification
```

Scenario:

A product or service carries a safety-related claim in Commercium.

Useful flow:

```text

→ required evidence grade

```

Finding:

> **Verified Provenance Is Not Verified Safety, and Verified Safety Is Not Universal Fitness.**

Risk if collapsed:

- authentic item assumed safe;
- passing one test assumed universally safe;
- assurance artifact substitutes for user-specific judgment.

Result: **very strong**.

Result: **very strong**.

# 12 — Creator Public Goods ↔ Market Assurance

Core coordination:

```text
shared infrastructure support
↔

```

Scenario:

Publicly supported creator infrastructure provides commerce, hosting, provenance, publishing, package, or distribution services.

Result: **very strong**.

# Audit synthesis

All twelve inner-octahedral neighbor relations produce distinct coordination problems.

This strengthens the interpretation of the inner octahedron as a **coordination topology**.

But it does **not** prove that twelve new runtime services or contracts should exist.

The correct implementation posture remains:

```text
semantic junction-neighbor relation
→ identify real workflow requirement
→ compose existing Edge Contract actions if needed
→ preserve authority at original systems
```

Candidate law:

> **Junction Coordination Is Derived, Not Sovereign — a relationship between two inner junctions may describe a real multi-system workflow, but it cannot create authority beyond the systems and contracts being composed.**

## Repeated junction-neighbor laws

1. **Witness–Intervention Separation**;
2. **Public Support Does Not Own Meaning**;
3. **Assurance Must Be Explainable Without Scope Expansion**;
4. **Witness Does Not Become Funding Authority**;
5. **Safety Exception Does Not Become General Standard**;
6. **Patronage Terms Must Declare Rights Scope**;
7. **Compliance Is Typed**;
8. **Public Infrastructure Requires Portability / Exit Boundaries**;
9. **Safety Governs Harmful Action, Not Symbolic Meaning**;
10. **Provenance ≠ Safety ≠ Universal Fitness**;
11. **Capability Support Must Not Become Cultural Ranking**;
12. **Public Funding Does Not Substitute for Assurance**.

## Structural conclusion

The current inner geometry now has evidence at three levels:

```text
LEVEL A
6 individual junctions
→ each has a differentiated coordination function

LEVEL B
3 antipodal tensions
→ each has a coherent deep system polarity

LEVEL C
12 neighboring junction relations
→ each exposes a distinct coordination boundary
```

This is a meaningful result.

It means the octahedron is not merely a collection of six convenient labels: its adjacency structure also produces useful architecture questions.

However:

> **Useful semantic adjacency remains insufficient evidence for literal runtime adjacency.**

`Semantic Edge ≠ Executable Edge` remains controlling.
