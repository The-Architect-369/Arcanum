# Classical Seven Lens-Model Test

## Purpose

This study runs the next falsification gate produced by the Classical Seven axis audit.

Instead of asking:

```text
Which planet permanently owns which coordinate?
```

it asks:

> **What happens when Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn are treated as selectable interpretive operators applied to an already-defined relationship?**

The test is run at two scales:

1. six concrete Hope A2 / Inner Portrait relationships;
2. all six ARCnet inner-octahedral coordination junctions.

The goal is to distinguish:

- genuinely new information;
- useful complementary framing;
- semantic restatement;
- risky or distorting interpretation.

No result establishes astrological causation, psychological type, metaphysical law, institutional authority, or human worth.

---

# Governing constraints

The following remain controlling:

```text
Identity is continuity, not description.
Hope may reflect but not diagnose, rank, command, or ratify.
Tempus may present optional archetypal symbolism but not celestial determinism.
Vitae recognition remains separate from reflective state.
A2 remains private, revisable, participant-owned, and non-essentialist.
Geometry creates no permission or authority.
Archetypal reflection creates no governance, security, economic, Treasury, or recognition status.
```

This study also inherits the prior findings:

- **Self-Similar Grammar, Not Self-Same Ontology**;
- **Scale-Dependent Orientation**;
- **Lens Before Label**;
- **Archetype Does Not Own Coordinate**;
- **Pairing Follows Relationship**;
- **Cross-Scale Non-Inference**.

# Lens vocabulary under test

The six non-solar classical bodies are treated as verbs/questions rather than personality identities.

```text
MOON
receive / remember / reflect / notice change

MERCURY
name / express / translate / negotiate / exchange

VENUS
value / relate / appreciate / harmonize / affiliate

MARS
act / assert / defend / separate / confront / risk

JUPITER
judge / contextualize / support / enlarge / seek broader meaning

SATURN
bound / structure / carry / limit / test / make durable
```

The Sun is intentionally withheld from the six-lens comparison because the current hypothesis places it at the center as an **integration/orientation symbol**, not as another lateral category.

---

# Evaluation notation

The notation below evaluates the **architecture question**, never a person.

```text
D — Distinct
    the lens exposes materially new information or a new decision/reflection dimension

C — Complementary
    useful, but overlaps significantly with information already exposed by the relationship

R — Restatement
    the lens mostly renames what the target relationship already means

B — Bounded-only
    can be useful, but carries a material risk of conflating affinity, legitimacy,
    worth, trust, authority, or another prohibited category unless strongly constrained
```

This is not a score, ranking, or optimization system.

# Part I — Hope A2 relationship tests

The six A2 cases intentionally include both proposed deep axes and ordinary neighboring relations.

```text
A1 Presence ↔ Expression
A2 Agency ↔ Relation
A3 Discernment ↔ Care
A4 Presence ↔ Agency
A5 Expression ↔ Relation
A6 Agency ↔ Care
```

These are design prompts derived from the current candidate A2 vocabulary. They remain replaceable and participant-editable.

## A1 — Presence ↔ Expression

Core relation:

```text
what is here now
↔
what seeks form, speech, action, or creation
```

### Moon — D

Questions:

- What are you receiving before you respond?
- What has changed since the last time you tried to express this?
- What memory or felt residue is shaping the present expression?

Distinct contribution:

Moon inserts **receptive temporality** between current state and outward form.

It prevents expression from being treated as a static output.

### Mercury — D

Questions:

- What exactly are you trying to say?
- What is observation versus interpretation?
- What may be lost between inner experience and outward language?

Distinct contribution:

Mercury tests the **translation boundary** between inner state and representation.

### Venus — C

Questions:

- What do you value in what is seeking expression?
- What kind of relation do you want the expression to create?
- What form feels consonant without requiring perfection?

Contribution:

Useful, but partly overlaps with the meaning already carried by Expression.

### Mars — D

Questions:

- What would it mean to actually act or speak?
- What risk accompanies expression?
- What boundary protects expression from becoming self-erasure or aggression?

Distinct contribution:

Mars separates **having an expression** from **crossing into action**.

### Jupiter — C

Questions:

- What larger meaning are you attaching to this expression?
- Does the significance you feel exceed the evidence available?
- What possibility might this expression responsibly enlarge?

Contribution:

Useful macro-context, but not always needed in a present-state reflection.

### Saturn — D

Questions:

- What constraint would make the expression clearer?
- What responsibility follows once it is made public or acted upon?
- What should remain private, unfinished, or bounded?

Distinct contribution:

Saturn introduces **durability, consequence, and explicit limits**.

### A1 result

```text
Moon D | Mercury D | Venus C | Mars D | Jupiter C | Saturn D
```

No single planet defines the relation.

Mercury is especially strong for translation, Mars for enactment, Moon for reception, and Saturn for consequence.

## A2 — Agency ↔ Relation

Core relation:

```text
self-direction
↔
mutual encounter
```

### Moon — C

Questions:

- What are you noticing yourself receive from the other person?
- What has changed in the relationship?
- Which old pattern is being reactivated, if any?

Useful receptive context, but not the central structural problem.

### Mercury — D

Questions:

- What has actually been communicated or agreed?
- Where are two people using the same word differently?
- What request needs clearer language?

Distinct contribution:

Mercury exposes **coordination through language**.

### Venus — D

Questions:

- What do you value about the relationship?
- What form of reciprocity feels voluntary rather than compulsory?
- What connection are you trying to preserve?

Distinct contribution:

Venus exposes **affinity, reciprocity, and relational value** without deciding action.

### Mars — D

Questions:

- Where does agency require a boundary?
- What action or refusal is actually yours to make?
- Where would avoiding conflict erase your own choice?

Distinct contribution:

Mars exposes **assertion and boundary**.

### Jupiter — C

Questions:

- What larger purpose does this relationship serve for you?
- What judgment are you making about what is fair or generous?
- Is a conflict being inflated into a universal conclusion?

Useful broad judgment, but can become over-interpretive if treated as moral authority.

### Saturn — D

Questions:

- What commitment has actually been made?
- What responsibility is yours, and what is not?
- What boundary must remain durable even when affection changes?

Distinct contribution:

Saturn separates **relationship from obligation** and obligation from unlimited duty.

### A2 result

```text
Moon C | Mercury D | Venus D | Mars D | Jupiter C | Saturn D
```

The previously proposed Venus ↔ Mars polarity remains especially legible, but Mercury and Saturn reveal equally necessary dimensions.

This argues against making Venus and Mars exclusive owners of the axis.

Questions:

### Venus — D

Questions:

- What is being valued or protected through care?

Distinct contribution:

### Mars — C

Questions:

- Does understanding require protective action?
- What harm calls for a boundary rather than further interpretation?
- Where would intervention itself create unnecessary force?

Contribution:

Useful when care crosses into action, otherwise secondary.

### Jupiter — D

Questions:

- What deserves support, and on what basis?

Distinct contribution:

Jupiter exposes the **judgment bridge** between knowing and deciding what matters.

### Saturn — D

Questions:

- What responsibility is actually sustainable?
- What limit prevents care from becoming total obligation?
- What evidence threshold should precede a durable conclusion?

Distinct contribution:

Saturn supplies **scope, burden, and durability tests**.

```text
Moon C | Mercury D | Venus D | Mars C | Jupiter D | Saturn D
```

This case is important because no single mythic opposition dominates it.

The palette behaves more like a set of orthogonal questions than a six-part personality taxonomy.

# A2 synthesis

Across the six A2 relationships, the lens model behaves usefully.

No archetype is universally dominant.

Several important patterns emerge.

## Pattern 1 — Lenses change the question, not the person

The same participant-owned relation can be interrogated through different functions without producing a stable type claim.

Example:

```text
Agency ↔ Relation

Mercury → coordination language
Venus   → relational value
Mars    → assertion/boundary
Saturn  → commitment/duty
```

The target remains the relationship.

The person does not become the archetype.

## Pattern 2 — Mercury and Saturn are broadly useful boundary lenses

Across several A2 cases:

- Mercury clarifies representation, naming, agreements, and translation;
- Saturn clarifies limits, commitment, responsibility, consequence, and durability.

This does **not** mean they are superior archetypes.

It means the current A2 relations frequently contain semantic and responsibility boundaries where those questions reveal additional information.

## Pattern 3 — Venus and Mars are especially useful in relational/action tensions

The Venus ↔ Mars polarity remains strongly legible in:

- Agency ↔ Relation;
- Expression ↔ Relation;
- Agency ↔ Care.

But the lens test shows that Mercury and Saturn often add information the Venus/Mars pair cannot provide alone.

## Pattern 4 — Moon prevents premature closure

Moon repeatedly asks what is being received or changing before the system converts experience into interpretation or action.

This function aligns closely with Hope's non-commanding posture.

## Pattern 5 — Jupiter is useful but requires proportion guards

Jupiter can widen local reflection into significance, judgment, support, or larger context.

Its failure mode is **inflation**:

```text
local feeling → universal meaning
personal judgment → moral truth
possible benefit → presumed legitimacy
```

Therefore Jupiter-like questions should be framed as invitations to examine scale, not declarations of meaning.

Core function:

```text
protected public-resource evidence
→ human-readable witness
→ understand / reflect / discuss / raise concern
without Treasury execution authority
```

### Moon — R

Question:

What happened, how is it being received, and what should be remembered?

- Does presentation preserve dignity and plural interpretation?

Useful human-context layer, but not the junction's primary architecture problem.

- Is concern being confused with governance authority?

Distinct public-meaning/proportion test.

### Saturn — D

- What evidence must be durable or auditable?

Distinct durable-constraint test.

# Inner-junction synthesis

The six junction tests produce a consistent and important result.

## Finding 1 — Resonance is not the same as interrogative utility

The original home correspondences remain semantically strong:

```text
Commons Witness      → Moon
Practice Safety      → Mars
Cultural Commons     → Venus
Market Assurance     → Mercury
Creator Public Goods → Jupiter
Standards and Rights → Saturn
```

But in **every case**, the home lens mostly restates the existing junction meaning.

This is not a failure of the correspondence.

It reveals two separate functions:

```text
ARCHETYPAL RESONANCE
human-readable mnemonic / symbolic atmosphere / orientation

ARCHETYPAL LENS
question that exposes a different dimension of the same relationship
```

Those functions should not be conflated.

## Finding 2 — Cross-lenses produce the architecture value

Examples:

```text
Commons Witness + Mercury
→ translation fidelity

Practice Safety + Saturn
→ exact policy scope

Cultural Commons + Mars
→ patronage/capture boundary

Market Assurance + Mars
→ adversarial threat model

Creator Public Goods + Saturn
→ maintenance / budget / sunset responsibility

Standards and Rights + Jupiter
→ legitimacy / proportionality of shared rule
```

The informational value comes from **rotation**, not permanent assignment.

## Finding 3 — Institutional context requires lens guards

The Market Assurance test exposes the strongest risk.

Venus-like affinity and Jupiter-like legitimacy can become dangerous if they leak into technical trust judgments.

Therefore:

> **A lens palette must be capability/domain-aware.**

A lens can be available for reflection while still being prohibited from producing certain institutional conclusions.

Candidate law:

> **Lens Output Constraint — the selected interpretive lens may change the question asked, but it may not change the destination's constitutional authority, evidence threshold, trust semantics, or permitted output type.**

## Finding 4 — Home correspondence can remain metadata, not authority

The six first-pass junction correspondences still have value as:

- optional iconography;
- symbolic orientation;
- mnemonic teaching language;
- Tempus/C2 cultural context;
- participant-selected visual atmosphere.

They should not become:

- fixed system identity;
- contract permission;
- default trust state;
- scoring dimension;
- governance category;
- Treasury classification;
- required UI vocabulary.

# Cross-scale comparison

## Hope A2

At the private reflective scale, almost every lens can usefully rotate across several relationships because the output remains a bounded question or participant-owned reflection.

The primary risks are:

- identity hardening;
- moralization;
- prediction;
- archetypal optimization;
- C2 symbolism dictating A2 state.

## ARCnet junctions

At the institutional/system scale, lens rotation is still valuable, but some lenses require tighter output guards because a reflective association could otherwise contaminate:

- trust;
- evidence;
- legitimacy;
- funding;
- rights;
- enforcement;
- safety.

Therefore the same lens vocabulary can cross scales only if the **output authority is scale-specific**.

Candidate law:

> **Shared Question, Different Authority — the same archetypal question may be posed at multiple scales, but the resulting artifact inherits only the authority of its local layer and target relationship.**

# Decision — palette, defaults, or fixed coordinates?

The evidence now supports a clear three-part decision.

## 1. Selectable lens palette — YES, leading model

The six bodies should primarily operate as a selectable question palette:

```text
Moon
Mercury
Venus
Mars
Jupiter
Saturn
```

A participant may choose a lens explicitly.

Hope may offer a lens as a transparent suggestion.

A system documentation/UI surface may explain how different lenses illuminate the same junction.

## 2. Default correspondences — YES, but only as non-exclusive resonance metadata

The current six junction correspondences may remain:

```text
J1 Moon
J2 Mars
J3 Venus
J4 Mercury
J5 Jupiter
J6 Saturn
```

but should be represented as something like:

```text
archetypal_resonance: optional
```

not:

```text
planetary_identity: fixed
```

The resonance can support teaching, iconography, or symbolic orientation.

It should not pre-answer the lens question.

## 3. Fixed planetary coordinate labels — NO for now

The test finds no architectural reason to permanently rename:

```text
A2 facets
or
ARCnet junction vertices
```

as planets.

Fixed naming would reduce useful rotational inquiry and increase the risk that symbolism is mistaken for ontology.

Therefore:

> **Do not promote planetary names into fixed A2 facet identities or fixed inner-junction system identities.**

# Suggested interaction rule

A future interface should begin in ordinary language.

Example:

```text
Agency ↔ Relation

Explore through another lens:
[ Moon ] [ Mercury ] [ Venus ] [ Mars ] [ Jupiter ] [ Saturn ]
```

Selecting Mercury might produce:

```text
What has actually been communicated or agreed?
```

Selecting Saturn might produce:

```text
What responsibility is actually yours, and what boundary must hold?
```

The same interaction grammar can appear on a system-junction explainer without importing private A2 data.

Example:

```text
Cultural Commons

View coordination risk through:
[ Mercury ] [ Mars ] [ Jupiter ] [ Saturn ] ...
```

The user should always be able to switch back to plain-language mode.

# Suggested-lens policy

A future Hope implementation may suggest a lens, but the suggestion must be based on **the selected relationship and explicit user intent**, not hidden personality inference.

Allowed:

```text
User is exploring Expression ↔ Relation.
Hope offers:
“Would a Mercury question about communication help?”
```

Not allowed:

```text
“Your history shows you are low in Mercury, so Mercury is recommended.”
```

Candidate law:

> **Relation-Based Suggestion, Not Person-Based Assignment — a lens suggestion may be derived from the current target relation or explicit participant request, never from a hidden planetary profile of the person.**

# Sun result — integration, not seventh analytic lens

With the six lateral lenses functioning as rotating questions, the Sun obtains a clearer role.

The Sun is best modeled as a **return-to-center / integration operation** rather than a seventh category competing with the other six.

At Hope A2 scale:

```text
After exploring several lenses:

SUN / CENTER
“What remains true for you to choose, name, or leave unresolved?”
```

At ARCnet system scale:

```text
After several coordination lenses:

CENTER CHECK
“What is the actual sovereign intent, authorized actor, and bounded action here?”
```

This preserves the prior doctrine:

```text
Sun may symbolize illumination/coherence.
Sun is not Identity itself.
```

Candidate law:

> **Solar Integration, Not Solar Typing — the solar symbol may mark return to the sovereign center and integration of perspectives; it does not define the person, grant authority, or create a seventh personality category.**

This produces a useful seven-part interaction grammar:

```text
6 lenses rotate around a relationship
+
1 center operation returns interpretation to sovereignty
```

without turning the seven into a human classification system.

# Candidate information model direction

This test is sufficient to distinguish three concepts that a later machine schema should keep separate:

```text
RELATION TARGET
what is being explored

ARCHETYPAL RESONANCE
optional symbolic association / teaching metadata

LENS INVOCATION
explicit application of one interpretive question family to the target
```

A later schema should also preserve:

```text
source / provenance
participant selection versus Hope suggestion
plain-language equivalent
allowed output class
prohibited inference classes
privacy / retention
no authority mutation
```

This document does not yet ratify a runtime schema.

# New candidate laws from the lens-model test

1. **Resonance Is Not Utility** — the archetype that best symbolizes a relationship may add the least new information when used as a question.
2. **Rotation Creates Insight** — archetypal value comes primarily from applying multiple bounded perspectives to an already-defined relationship.
3. **Lens Output Constraint** — a lens may change the question, never the target layer's evidence threshold, authority, trust semantics, or permitted output type.
4. **Shared Question, Different Authority** — the same lens may appear at private and institutional scales while its output remains constrained by local authority.
5. **Relation-Based Suggestion, Not Person-Based Assignment** — lens suggestions derive from explicit target relations/intents, never hidden personality profiles.
6. **Resonance Metadata, Not Coordinate Identity** — default correspondences may assist teaching or visualization without owning the coordinate.
7. **Plain-Language Primacy** — every archetypal interaction must have a non-symbolic equivalent and remain fully usable without planetary vocabulary.
8. **Solar Integration, Not Solar Typing** — Sun symbolizes return-to-center/integration, not Identity definition or a seventh trait.
9. **Institutional Lens Guarding** — lenses that could conflate affinity, legitimacy, popularity, value, or trust require explicit domain-level output prohibitions.
10. **No Archetypal Completion State** — using all six lenses is never required, rewarded, scored, or interpreted as personal/system completion.

# Final verdict

The lens-model test **passes**.

But it passes in a form materially different from fixed planetary geometry.

The leading architecture is now:

```text
SOVEREIGN CENTER
Identity / Self
       │
       │  return / integrate
       │  optional solar symbolism
       ▼
RELATION TARGET
A2 relation or ARCnet junction
       │
       ├─ Moon question
       ├─ Mercury question
       ├─ Venus question
       ├─ Mars question
       ├─ Jupiter question
       └─ Saturn question
```

with:

```text
planetary resonance = optional metadata
planetary lens = explicit interpretive operation
planetary identity = rejected
planetary authority = prohibited
```

The strongest discovery is:

> **The classical seven appear more architecturally useful as six rotating relational lenses plus one sovereign return-to-center symbol than as seven fixed categories of person or system.**

This preserves the intuition of a sevenfold visible celestial grammar while keeping the Arcanum aligned with Identity sovereignty, Hope non-authority, Tempus metaphysical neutrality, and the evidence-first architecture method.
