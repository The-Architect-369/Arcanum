# ARCnet Edge Study 05 — Nexus ↔ Theatrum

## Purpose

This study examines the candidate relationship between **Nexus** and **Theatrum** within the generative/lived tetrahedron.

It asks how digital creation, symbolic expression, collaborative authorship, publication, audience, remix, and cultural circulation should interact without turning audience response into authorship authority or popularity into truth.

## Semantic correspondence

Nexus asks:

> What do I place between myself and others, and with whom?

Theatrum asks:

> What form can an idea, identity, memory, or practice take as a digital or symbolic artifact?

The edge is therefore the threshold between:

- creation and publication;
- artifact and audience;
- private expression and social circulation;
- collaboration and authorship;
- cultural meaning and social response.

Candidate summary:

> **Theatrum gives expression form; Nexus gives expression relation and audience.**

## Core flow

A Theatrum artifact should not automatically become social content merely because it exists.

Candidate flow:

```text
Theatrum private artifact
        |
        | creator selects representation
        v
NexusPublicationArtifact
        |
        v
Nexus private draft
        |
        | audience + publication authorization
        v
Nexus social object
```

Reverse:

```text
Nexus post / discussion / collaboration request
        |
        | explicit create/remix action
        v
TheatrumCreationSeed
        |
        v
Theatrum private creative draft
```

The destination creates its own object rather than acquiring broad source access.

## Creation–Publication Separation

This edge generalizes Two-Phase Passage into a more specific cultural rule:

> **Creating an artifact and publishing an artifact are separate acts.**

A work may be:

- private;
- shared with selected collaborators;
- shown to a small circle;
- released publicly;
- licensed for remix;
- listed commercially;

without those states collapsing into one another.

## Audience response does not rewrite the artifact's authority

Nexus may carry:

- comments;
- reactions;
- discussion;
- curation;
- community interpretation.

Those responses do not automatically change:

- authorship;
- provenance;
- original artifact content;
- creator intent;
- Vitae meaning;
- licensing terms.

Candidate law:

> **Audience response may surround an artifact without becoming its provenance or its creator's identity.**

## Optional sovereign faculty projections

### Hope projection

Potential legitimate uses:

- selected creator statement;
- reflective caption;
- meaning or intention the creator chooses to disclose;
- a private reflection seed from audience response.

Hope must not ingest audience metrics automatically to optimize the creator for engagement.

### Tempus projection

Potential legitimate uses:

- creation date/window;
- scheduled release;
- event context;
- selected seasonal/ritual correspondence;
- time-bounded collaborative session.

Tempus must not manufacture urgency or imply that a release window determines artistic truth.

### Vitae projection

Potential legitimate uses:

- selected practice/learning provenance;
- a creator-chosen developmental source for an artifact;
- factual capability evidence for specialized collaborative tools.

Vitae must not become a public prestige meter attached to every creative work.

Again, the three faculties can participate together without any one being the edge itself.

## Authorship and collaboration

Theatrum ↔ Nexus makes collaborative authorship especially important.

A social conversation may lead to a co-created artifact, but conversational participation is not automatically authorship.

Candidate distinctions:

```text
conversation participant
idea contributor
source provider
editor
co-author
publisher
curator
license holder
```

These roles may overlap but should not be silently inferred from one another.

Candidate law:

> **Participation in discussion does not automatically grant authorship, ownership, or publication authority.**

## Remix boundary

A Nexus user may encounter an artifact and choose:

```text
Remix in Theatrum
```

The edge should first inspect:

- whether remix is permitted;
- what source material may be copied;
- what attribution/provenance must be preserved;
- whether the result remains private or may later be published.

The resulting remix is a new Theatrum artifact with lineage, not a silent mutation of the original.

Candidate law:

> **Remix creates lineage, not replacement.**

## Cultural circulation versus economic transfer

A Nexus publication may circulate freely while the underlying artifact has separate licensing or commercial terms.

Likewise, an artifact may be sold through Commercium without granting unlimited social publication rights.

This reinforces non-transitive edge authority:

```text
Theatrum -> Nexus publication permission
!=
Theatrum -> Commercium sale/license permission
```

Each relationship needs its own authorization.

## Social metrics boundary

Metrics such as views, reactions, reposts, or comments may help a creator understand circulation, but they should not automatically become:

- Vitae recognition;
- Hope psychological modeling;
- creator worth;
- economic entitlement;
- governance authority.

A participant may deliberately bring selected audience feedback into Hope or another private reflection context.

## New patterns extracted

1. **Creation–Publication Separation** — artifact existence does not imply social release.
2. **Audience–Provenance Separation** — reactions and interpretation do not rewrite authorship or origin.
3. **Role Separation** — discussion, contribution, authorship, curation, and publication authority are distinct.
4. **Remix Lineage** — derivative creation preserves lineage rather than silently replacing source artifacts.
5. **Cross-Edge License Separation** — social publication permission does not imply sale/remix permission.
6. **Multi-Faculty Context** — Hope, Tempus, and Vitae can all legitimately contribute bounded optional context.

## Open questions

- Should Nexus host galleries itself or only social references to Theatrum galleries?
- What minimum lineage metadata should survive every remix?
- How should collaborative authorship be explicitly accepted by all named authors?
- Which licensing controls belong in Theatrum, Commercium, or Protection?
- How should deleted social publications relate to persistent private artifacts and peer-held copies?
- Should audience analytics be local-first summaries rather than centralized creator dossiers?
