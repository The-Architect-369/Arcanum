# ARCnet Edge Study 18 — Nexus ↔ Aerarium

## Candidate meaning

> **Community Need ↔ Public Goods**

Nexus can surface community needs, project ideas, stewardship gaps, and public-benefit proposals. Aerarium can expose bounded funding pathways under Treasury and governance authority.

## Strongest law — Popularity May Surface Need; It Must Not Allocate the Commons by Itself

> **Engagement, follower count, or social reach may not become direct Treasury allocation authority.**

## Candidate flow

```text
Nexus community signal
      |
      | bounded proposal / evidence packet
      v
Public-Goods Request
      |
      v
Aerarium review / funding pathway
      |
      | separate governance + Treasury controls
      v
bounded allocation
```

## Candidate laws

1. **Popularity–Allocation Separation** — social reach is not spending authority.
2. **Community Evidence Without Dossiers** — proposals may cite need without exposing unrelated participant histories.
3. **Conflict Disclosure** — social organizers requesting resources should disclose relevant conflicts without surrendering full financial privacy.
4. **Transparent Outcome** — funding state should be publicly reconstructable from authorization to execution.
5. **No Pay-to-Reach-to-Fund Loop** — MANA spending must not buy social amplification that mechanically converts into public funding priority.

## Orientation result

This relationship is highly meaningful as an operational cube edge, but weaker as a mirror than Nexus ↔ Imperium. Nexus gathers voices; Aerarium routes common resources. The two interact, but they are not the same phenomenon in opposite form.
