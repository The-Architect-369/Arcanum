# ARCnet Edge Study 03 — Arcanum ↔ Theatrum

## Purpose

This study tests the candidate relationship between **Arcanum** and **Theatrum** within the generative/lived tetrahedron.

It asks whether a person's lived development, practice, and stabilized capacities can legitimately inform symbolic or digital expression without allowing art, cosmetics, ownership, popularity, or payment to become a substitute for becoming itself.

This document is design evidence only.

## Repository grounding

Current canon gives this edge a strong dignity boundary:

- `docs/modules/vitae/vitae-and-becoming.md` defines Vitae as a recognition layer of becoming, not a reputation score, leaderboard, social-credit system, or measure of human worth.
- Vitae follows a stability-first posture: becoming stabilizes first, then may be named.
- Vitae recognition may support bounded capability decisions but must not become purchasable status or social supremacy.
- The Economic Constitution explicitly forbids MANA from purchasing Vitae recognition and states that payment may expand capacity but may not accelerate becoming.
- The current project-status record does not describe a production Theatrum application. Theatrum is therefore a new design-candidate system in this exploration.

The Theatrum-facing structures below are hypotheses to test, not existing canon.

## Semantic correspondence

Arcanum asks:

> What have I lived, practiced, integrated, or become?

Theatrum asks:

> What may I create, symbolize, inhabit, perform, or display from that experience?

The candidate edge is the threshold between:

- becoming and expression;
- lived practice and symbolic form;
- recognition and representation;
- inner development and digital embodiment;
- experience and aesthetic creation.

A concise candidate description is:

> **Arcanum gives lived meaning to expression; Theatrum gives perceivable form to lived meaning.**

## Candidate endpoint faculties

### Arcanum-facing port: Vitae

Vitae is the leading Arcanum-facing faculty because this edge concerns how lived formation may be represented after it exists.

Vitae should expose only bounded, consented facts or symbols suitable for expression.

It must not give Theatrum access to a complete developmental history, private proofs, or hidden human-worth model.

### Theatrum-facing port: Studio / Persona / World

Theatrum does not yet have a canonical module taxonomy.

For this study, its facing faculty is provisionally described as:

```text
Studio
  + Asset / Artifact Creation
  + Persona / Presentation
  + Environment / World
```

These are capability families, not ratified module names.

## The central distinction: Recognition versus Representation

This edge exposes a critical semantic separation.

A **Vitae recognition** is a bounded statement about stabilized capacity or development under Vitae's rules.

A **Theatrum representation** is an expressive artifact.

They are not the same object.

Candidate flow:

```text
Vitae record / practice / recognized transition
      private Arcanum source
             |
             | person chooses what may become visible
             v
ArcanumTheatrumExpressionSeed
      bounded derivative
             |
             v
Theatrum Creative Draft
             |
             | user creates / edits
             v
Digital Artifact / Persona Element / Environment
             |
             | optional separate release
             v
Published / shared / equipped expression
```

This reinforces the Derived Artifact Law.

The source recognition remains in Arcanum.

Theatrum receives an **expression seed**, not ownership over the recognition itself.

## Representation Independence Law

A person must be allowed to create a representation that is inspired by their journey without making the representation authoritative evidence of that journey.

For example:

- a participant may create a visual mantle inspired by a completed practice;
- a participant may build a room representing a stage of learning;
- a participant may create art based on a Vitae path;
- a participant may choose a symbol associated with a personal transition.

But the resulting object is not itself proof that the underlying recognition exists.

Candidate law:

> **Representation may reference recognition; representation must not become recognition.**

This becomes especially important when digital artifacts can be copied, traded, gifted, remixed, or purchased.

## Cosmetic versus Capability boundary

This study strongly suggests that Theatrum should distinguish **cosmetic expression** from **system capability**.

A visual artifact may look like a robe, badge, sigil, tool, room, title treatment, aura, card frame, avatar layer, or world object.

That appearance must not silently grant the system permissions that the participant does not otherwise hold.

```text
visible artifact
    !=
capability grant
    !=
Vitae recognition
    !=
governance authority
```

A purchased or transferred cosmetic should therefore not confer:

- a Vitae grade;
- responsibility eligibility;
- governance weight;
- protected tool access;
- human-worth status;
- identity legitimacy.

Candidate law:

> **Visual possession and visual resemblance do not confer underlying authority.**

## Expression Seed — candidate artifact

An Arcanum → Theatrum Expression Seed might contain explicitly selected information such as:

```text
source type
- practice
- completed lesson
- recognized transition
- chosen personal symbol
- participant-authored narrative

presentation context
- title chosen for this artifact
- palette / symbolic grammar
- selected iconography
- optional provenance reference

privacy
- whether the underlying source is named
- whether the relationship to Vitae is public
- whether the artifact appears purely artistic
```

The participant may choose to reveal very little.

For example, a digital artwork may be informed by a private Vitae event while exposing no public statement that the event occurred.

This reveals a candidate pattern:

> **A derived artifact may preserve private causation locally while presenting only chosen meaning publicly.**

## Disclosure modes

The edge may need multiple disclosure modes.

### Opaque inspiration

Theatrum receives enough local context to help create the artifact, but the final artifact does not expose its Arcanum source.

```text
"Inspired by my practice"
```

with no public Vitae reference.

### Declared inspiration

The artifact publicly states that it was inspired by a selected Arcanum practice or path.

### Verified reference

The artifact includes a narrow factual reference to a valid recognition receipt or practice receipt where doing so is appropriate and consented.

Even here, the artifact does not become the underlying receipt.

These modes should not be collapsed into one default behavior.

## Theatrum can embody without exposing

This edge reveals an important privacy-friendly design opportunity.

A participant could let Arcanum influence their personal Theatrum environment entirely locally.

Example:

```text
Vitae transition
      |
      | local-only expression seed
      v
Theatrum environment changes
- new visual motif
- new room element
- altered card frame
- new soundscape
```

No social publication is required.

No chain settlement is required.

No one else needs to know which Vitae event caused the change.

This means Theatrum may become one of the strongest places where the system can make development **perceptible** without making development **performative**.

Candidate principle:

> **Embodiment may be private. Visibility is not a prerequisite for meaning.**

## The danger of performative becoming

Vitae canon explicitly warns against performative growth: people optimizing for badges rather than becoming.

Theatrum could accidentally recreate that problem if visual prestige becomes tightly coupled to public Vitae signaling.

Dangerous pattern:

```text
more advanced-looking cosmetic
        -> assumed higher human status
        -> social prestige
        -> pressure to optimize for display
```

The architecture should resist that loop.

Possible safeguards include:

- many expressive options not tied to Vitae at all;
- no globally ordered rarity hierarchy representing human development;
- no requirement to publicly display recognition;
- participant choice to render recognition abstractly or privately;
- purchased cosmetics visually rich but never equivalent to Vitae authority;
- no automatic social ranking from equipped artifacts.

This suggests a broader law:

> **Embodiment must not turn protected development into a prestige market.**

## Economics boundary

Arcanum → Theatrum passage should ordinarily have no economic consequence for local personal expression.

Theatrum may later support legitimate economic actions such as:

- buying creator-made cosmetic assets;
- commissioning digital art;
- paying for scarce rendering/storage capacity;
- licensing or transferring eligible digital creations;
- supporting creators through MANA.

But the Economic Constitution remains controlling:

- MANA may support creator economies;
- MANA may not purchase Vitae recognition;
- MANA may not accelerate becoming;
- economic consequences must be visible before authorization;
- wealth may not become human-worth status.

A useful hard rule for this edge is:

> **The expression may be bought; the becoming cannot.**

If a creator sells a visual representation inspired by a Vitae archetype, the buyer acquires the permitted artifact/license—not the seller's or archetype's Vitae recognition.

## Transfer and provenance

Digital artifacts may be transferable even when the underlying Arcanum source is not.

For example:

```text
Vitae source
   non-transferable personal context
        |
        v
Expression Seed
        |
        v
Digital Artifact
   potentially transferable under Theatrum rules
```

This yields another candidate general law:

> **Transferability belongs to the derivative's contract, not automatically to the source object's properties.**

Conversely, a non-transferable recognition may legitimately inspire a transferable artwork without transferring the recognition.

The reverse is also possible: a Theatrum artifact may be intentionally identity-bound or non-transferable even if it has no Vitae authority.

## Authorship and provenance

Theatrum should preserve relevant creative provenance such as:

- participant-created;
- collaboratively created;
- assistant-assisted;
- imported asset;
- derived from another licensed artifact;
- optionally inspired by a selected Arcanum source.

As with Edge Study 01, authorship provenance is about integrity, not ranking creativity.

Private Arcanum identifiers should remain local unless explicitly disclosed.

A public artifact might reveal only:

```text
creator: selected identity/pseudonym
creation digest: ...
license: ...
source inspiration: optional
verified reference: optional narrow receipt
```

## Two-phase creation and release

The Arcanum ↔ Nexus edge separated passage from social publication.

Theatrum reveals a similar but slightly different sequence:

```text
Arcanum source
    |
    v
Expression Seed
    |
    v
Theatrum Draft
    |
    | create / render / edit
    v
Completed Artifact
    |
    | separate decision
    +--> keep private
    +--> equip locally
    +--> share through Nexus
    +--> list through Commercium
    +--> publish in Theatrum
```

This is an important graph pattern.

A completed digital artifact can become an input to several later edges, but its completion does not authorize any of those crossings.

This reinforces **Non-Transitive Edge Authority** from Edge Study 02.

## Relationship to Nexus and Commercium

Theatrum should not absorb functions belonging to neighboring systems merely because a digital object is involved.

Candidate division:

- **Arcanum ↔ Theatrum** — lived meaning → digital/symbolic expression.
- **Theatrum ↔ Nexus** — creation → social publication/exhibition/community.
- **Theatrum ↔ Commercium** — digital artifact ↔ physical twin, licensing, sale, or exchange.
- **Theatrum ↔ Protection** — integrity, package/artifact safety, provenance, rights, and authenticity checks.

Example:

```text
Vitae practice
    |
Arcanum ↔ Theatrum
    |
private digital artifact
    |
    +-- Theatrum ↔ Nexus --> share publicly
    |
    +-- Theatrum ↔ Commercium --> sell/license
```

The first edge does not grant permission for the later edges.

## Reverse direction — Theatrum → Arcanum

A digital artwork, environment, symbol, avatar element, or interactive world may become meaningful input for private reflection or practice.

Theatrum should not automatically write aesthetic choices into Vitae.

A participant may explicitly choose actions such as:

```text
Reflect on this in Arcanum
Use this as a practice symbol
Add this to my private path materials
```

Candidate flow:

```text
Theatrum Artifact
      |
      | explicit participant choice
      v
ArcanumExpressionReference
      |
      +--> Hope reflection seed
      +--> Tempus symbolic/practice reference
      +--> Vitae learning/practice material
```

The imported artifact does not automatically create Vitae recognition.

Watching a film, equipping an avatar, owning a collectible, or inhabiting a world is not itself evidence of stabilized development unless a separately valid Vitae process establishes something relevant.

## Patterns extracted from edge study 03

### Reconfirmed from earlier studies

1. **Derived Artifact Law** — Theatrum receives an expression seed, not broad access to Vitae.
2. **Two-Phase Passage** — passage into Theatrum and later release/use are separate.
3. **Monotonic Disclosure** — private developmental context does not become public implicitly.
4. **Authority Conservation** — representation does not gain Vitae authority.
5. **Provenance Without Surveillance** — creative provenance need not expose a developmental dossier.
6. **Destination Economics** — local passage is not monetized; Theatrum-native scarce utility or exchange may be.
7. **Transport Independence** — local/private embodiment must not depend on a particular network transport.
8. **Non-Transitive Edge Authority** — finished art does not automatically authorize Nexus publication or Commercium sale.

### Newly exposed

9. **Recognition–Representation Separation** — a representation may reference recognition but must never become the recognition itself.
10. **Cosmetic–Capability Separation** — visual possession, appearance, or rarity does not grant underlying system capability.
11. **Private Embodiment Principle** — lived development may become perceptible to the participant without becoming socially visible.
12. **Anti-Prestige Boundary** — embodiment must not convert development into a public status economy.
13. **Derivative Transferability Law** — transferability is defined by the derivative artifact's contract, not inherited automatically from the source.
14. **Private Causation / Public Meaning Separation** — a public artifact may expose chosen meaning while the private reason or source that inspired it remains local.

## Comparative pattern across the first three Arcanum edges

A notable structure is emerging:

```text
Arcanum -> Nexus
private meaning -> communicable relation
primary faculty candidate: Hope

Arcanum -> Commercium
private/selected correspondence -> material craft
primary faculty candidate: Tempus

Arcanum -> Theatrum
lived becoming -> symbolic/digital expression
primary faculty candidate: Vitae
```

All three currently preserve the same deep rule:

```text
source state
   -> bounded derivative
   -> destination draft/object
   -> destination-native action
```

But each stresses a different boundary:

- Nexus stresses **privacy and audience**.
- Commercium stresses **physical evidence, provenance, and economic exchange**.
- Theatrum stresses **representation, embodiment, prestige, and transferability**.

That diversity makes these three useful tests of the common ARCnet Edge Contract rather than three copies of the same interaction.

## Open questions

- Is Vitae permanently the primary Arcanum-facing port for Theatrum, or should some purely artistic actions originate from Hope or Tempus without passing through Vitae?
- What should the first Theatrum object model be: asset, card, scene, room, avatar layer, world object, or a more general artifact primitive?
- Which Theatrum artifacts should be transferable, identity-bound, copyable, remixable, or licensable?
- How should the system visually distinguish a purchased cosmetic from a Vitae-referenced embodiment so participants do not infer false authority?
- Should a verified Vitae reference be visible by default, hidden by default, or always a separate user choice?
- How much private source provenance should remain on-device when a public artifact is created?
- Can Theatrum provide personal environmental changes from Hope/Tempus/Vitae without creating covert behavioral profiling?
- Where should licensing and creator-rights enforcement live: Theatrum, Commercium, Protection, or a composition of those edges?

## Next gate

Compare the first three Arcanum kinship edges as a set.

If Hope ↔ Nexus, Tempus ↔ Commercium, and Vitae ↔ Theatrum continue to hold, the next useful design step is to formalize the emerging **Arcanum three-port model** and then explore one of the three base edges among Nexus, Commercium, and Theatrum to determine whether the same edge laws survive when **Arcanum is no longer one of the endpoints**.
