# Vitae Icosahedron Geometry Audit

## Purpose

This audit tests whether the regular icosahedron should become:

1. the fixed semantic architecture of Vitae;
2. a bounded navigation geometry for the Vitae corpus;
3. a path / journey representation;
4. or none of the above.

The Human Architect's intuition under test is that Vitae behaves less like a static calendar and more like a **river, path, jewel, or many-faceted corpus of becoming**.

The audit therefore asks not only whether the number `20` appears, but whether the **thirty adjacencies**, **ten opposite-face pairs**, **triangular local structure**, and **closed surface** preserve the actual Vitae doctrine.

# 2. Geometry under test

A regular icosahedron has:

```text
20 triangular faces
12 vertices
30 edges
```

If **faces** are treated as Vitae corpus regions, face adjacency has the topology of the dodecahedral graph:

```text
20 face-regions
3 neighboring faces per face
30 face-adjacency relations
1 unique opposite face per face
10 opposite-face pairs
```

From one face, the face-relation distance shells are:

```text
self              1
adjacent ring      3
second ring        6
third ring         6
fourth ring        3
opposite           1
```

This produces a natural visual sense of branching outward from a selected face.

That visual property is compatible with the "river / jewel / path" intuition.

It does not yet prove curriculum semantics.

The Vitae corpus contains a genuine twenty-unit high-level set:

```text
10 Core Vitae grades

10 Specializations
=
20 named curriculum regions
```

```text
I    Guardian
II   Seeker
III  Disciple
IV   Mystic
V    Scholar
VI   Healer
VII  Alchemist
VIII Sage
IX   Oracle
X    Adept
```

## Ten specializations

```text
Arcanist
Philosopher
Illusionist
Astrologer
Hierophant
Druid
Necromancer
Alchemist
Artificer
Enchanter
```

This is not an invented count.

It is already present in the repository.

Finding:

> **Twenty faces as a high-level Vitae corpus index — COUNT TEST PASSES.**

This is the strongest positive evidence for the icosahedron.

But the twenty units are not homogeneous.

```text
grades
= staged responsibility / formation architecture

specializations
= non-hierarchical advanced stewardship domains
```

Therefore count equivalence does not automatically make them one uniform graph.

Candidate law:

> **Corpus Count Does Not Grant Edge Meaning — a face count may organize named regions without authorizing the geometric edges between those regions.**

# 4. Vitae contains more than the twenty named regions

The corpus also contains structural layers that are not members of the `10 + 10` set:

The twenty faces therefore cannot truthfully mean:

```text

= metadata, overlays, paths, thresholds, receipts, review, and doctrine surrounding the atlas
```

Finding: **icosahedron as complete Vitae ontology — FAIL.**

Finding: **icosahedron as primary curriculum atlas — still viable.**

# 5. Can ten grade faces preserve the Grade I → X path?

The face-adjacency graph contains induced paths of ten faces.

Therefore it is mathematically possible to place:

```text
Guardian → Seeker → Disciple → Mystic → Scholar
→ Healer → Alchemist → Sage → Oracle → Adept
```

on ten faces such that the grade faces have exactly nine grade-to-grade adjacency edges and no extra grade-to-grade chords.

This initially looks promising.

However, because every icosahedral face has degree three, a ten-face induced grade path necessarily creates:

```text
9  grade ↔ grade edges
12 grade ↔ specialization edges
9  specialization ↔ specialization edges
=
30 total face-adjacency edges
```

The count is forced by the cubic face-adjacency topology.

Those twelve cross-family edges are the problem.

The current corpus does **not** define twelve privileged relationships of the form:

```text
this particular grade
↔
this particular specialization
```

Specializations are described as post-Core, non-hierarchical, non-exclusive stewardship paths.

A specialization does not become legitimate because it happens to touch one or two grade faces in a solid.

Finding:

> **A ten-face grade path can be embedded, but doing so fabricates twelve grade–specialization adjacencies and nine privileged specialization adjacencies.**

Result: **fixed semantic 20-face dependency graph — FAIL.**

Candidate law:

> **Core Completion Is Global, Not Spoke-Based — a specialization's relationship to Core Vitae may not be reduced to whichever grade face happens to touch it geometrically.**

# 6. The seductive 10 + 5 + 5 decomposition

The twenty-face adjacency graph admits a particularly striking decomposition.

There are valid ways to select ten matching edges so that the remaining twenty adjacency edges form:

```text
one 10-face cycle
+
one 5-face cycle
+
one 5-face cycle
```

with the removed ten edges acting like connecting spokes.

Visually this could tempt an architecture such as:

```text
10-cycle
= Grades I–X

5-cycle
= five specializations

5-cycle
= five specializations

10 spokes
= grade ↔ specialization correspondences
```

The count is beautiful.

The semantics fail.

It would invent three things the Vitae corpus does not currently say:

1. **Adept → Guardian closure.** The 10-cycle adds an adjacency from the end of the grade ladder back to its beginning.
2. **Two canonical specialization pentads.** The ten specializations are explicitly non-hierarchical and no repository source divides them into two privileged groups of five.
3. **One-to-one grade / specialization spokes.** No canonical pairing exists.

Finding: **10 + 5 + 5 decomposition — mathematically elegant, doctrinally unsupported.**

Candidate law:

> **Elegant Decomposition Is Still a Hypothesis — a graph factorization cannot create curriculum families, progression loops, or pairings absent from the source architecture.**

# 7. Ten opposite-face pairs

Twenty triangular faces create ten unique opposite-face pairs.

This invites several possible mappings.

## Candidate A — one grade opposite one specialization

```text
10 grades
↔
10 specializations
```

This would create ten mirror pairs.

The current corpus does not define those pairings.

Several thematic pairings can be imagined, but they are not unique.

Examples of ambiguous resonance:

```text
Scholar could resonate with Arcanist
Mystic could resonate with Illusionist
Oracle could resonate with Astrologer
Healer could resonate with Druid
Alchemist could resonate with Alchemist
```

but other reasonable pairings are equally possible.

A geometric opposite must not become curricular doctrine by aesthetic preference.

Result: **grade ↔ specialization opposite pairs — FAIL / undefined.**

## Candidate B — each grade uses an opposite pair

Another possibility is:

```text
10 grades × 2 complementary faces = 20 faces
```

Possible dualities might include:

```text
capacity ↔ responsibility
practice ↔ recognition
formation ↔ entrusted consequence
```

This has conceptual resonance with Vitae's stability-first posture.

However:

- it excludes the ten specializations from the face registry;
- the existing grade architecture historically defines four mandatory artifacts, not a canonical two-face division;
- opposite faces are maximally separated in the face graph, which does not naturally express a tightly coupled two-part grade object.

Result: **grade dual-face model — HOLD as a future hypothesis, not passed.**

Candidate law:

> **Opposite Pairing Requires Doctrine — geometric antipodes may visualize an already-defined duality; they may not manufacture one.**

# 8. Twelve icosahedral vertices

The audit searched Vitae for a canonical twelve-part set.

No current Vitae registry contains twelve peer structural positions.

Tempting constructions include:

```text
10 grades
+
entry threshold
+

=
```

or similar combinations.

These combine different structural roles and are not defined as one twelvefold architecture.

Other Vitae counts include:

```text

4 mandatory grade artifacts in the historical grade template
```

None independently validates twelve vertices.

Finding: **twelve Vitae vertices as semantic nodes — FAIL / undefined.**

Candidate law:

> **Do Not Complete the Solid by Count Rescue — unassigned vertices may remain unassigned rather than combining unlike curriculum concepts to reach twelve.**

# 9. Triangular faces versus Vitae artifact structure

Every icosahedral face is triangular.

This invites a local three-part interpretation.

The current Vitae architecture does contain some three-part descriptions at the module level, such as:

```text
Authority
Constitution
Curriculum
```

But those are global Vitae surfaces, not three local components of each grade or specialization.

The historical Grade Architecture Template instead specifies four mandatory artifacts:

```text
Functional Outcomes Manifest
Entry Threshold Codex
Internal Agreements
Exit Threshold Codex
```

Current app-facing documents further distinguish:

```text
content
practice
review
permission / authority
privacy / receipt context
```

Therefore the triangle's three corners do not currently map cleanly to the internal architecture of a grade or specialization.

Finding: **triangular face as literal grade artifact schema — FAIL.**

A triangular face may still be a visual card/chamber without semantic corner assignments.

# 10. Degree-three adjacency versus actual dependency structure

Every face has exactly three adjacent faces.

The current Vitae relationships are not degree-three.

Examples:

- higher grades inherit broad foundations from multiple earlier grades;
- current cross-grade documents contain non-local dependency rules;
- specializations are related to the completed Core rather than exactly three curriculum neighbors;
- multiple specializations may be pursued concurrently;
- cross-specialization mastery overlays can involve three or more paths;
- authority envelopes are separately reviewed and not produced by adjacency.

Therefore:

```text
three geometric neighbors
≠
three curricular prerequisites
≠
three safe capabilities
≠
three mastery relations
```

Finding: **face adjacency as dependency — FAIL.**

Candidate law:

> **Dependency Degree Is Data, Not Geometry — curriculum prerequisites must be generated from the actual Vitae dependency registry and may not be limited to a polyhedron's fixed vertex degree.**

# 11. Specialization relationship falsification

The Specialization Overview states that the ten paths are:

```text
non-hierarchical
non-exclusive
counterbalancing
no required progression order
```

An icosahedral face has only three immediate neighbors.

If adjacency were interpreted as strong semantic relation, each specialization would appear specially related to three nearby regions and less related to the remaining six or more.

No current specialization canon justifies such a fixed degree-three neighborhood.

This could accidentally create:

- artificial schools or factions;
- preferred combination paths;
- implied incompatibilities;
- hidden progression orders;
- prestige around central-looking routes.

Finding: **fixed specialization adjacency — FAIL until derived from corpus evidence.**

Candidate law:

> **Specialization Adjacency Is Not Exclusivity — spatial closeness may not imply compatibility, priority, prerequisite, or superiority among non-hierarchical paths.**

# 12. Path / river test

The icosahedron still performs well as a **path visualization** if the path is treated as an overlay rather than an intrinsic edge law.

A participant-selected route could appear as:

```text
selected curriculum region
→ related actual prerequisite or practice reference
→ next participant-visible region
```

while unselected geometric edges remain inert.

This supports the Human Architect's "river" intuition:

```text
solid = corpus terrain
path = lived route through terrain
water = current movement / attention
recognition = separate event after stabilization
```

The path must remain:

- participant-visible;
- non-comparative;
- non-percentage-based;
- non-speed-scored;
- interruptible;
- revisable;
- capable of silence.

Candidate law:

> **The Map Is Not the Journey — a fixed corpus geometry may orient the participant while the actual path remains contingent, voluntary, and represented by separately derived curriculum data.**

Finding: **icosahedron as path terrain — PASS, bounded.**

# 13. Closed surface versus open becoming

An icosahedron is a closed surface.

Vitae explicitly rejects a final human completion state.

A closed visual object can easily suggest:

```text
complete all faces
fill the jewel
reach 100%
finish becoming
```

That would be incompatible with:

- silence as valid;
- non-participation legitimacy;
- no final mastery state;
- non-comparative recognition;
- no gamified progression pressure.

Therefore, if an icosahedron is used, **visual completeness must not mean human completion**.

Faces may remain unopened, irrelevant, private, or intentionally unpursued indefinitely.

Candidate law:

> **Closed Surface, Open Journey — geometric closure describes the map boundary, never completion of the being.**

Candidate law:

> **No Geometric Progress Meter — filled faces, brightness, area, volume, or percentage of a solid may not become a Vitae worth/readiness/progress score.**

# 14. Recognition boundary

A face may represent:

```text
available curriculum
selected practice domain
reference content
recognized responsibility envelope only after separate review
```

but merely navigating to or illuminating a face must never mean:

```text
recognized
ready
advanced
trusted
superior
authoritative
```

Current `VitaeContext` already preserves this distinction through local practice state and separately sourced authority state.

Candidate law:

> **Coordinate Is Not Recognition — occupancy, selection, traversal, or visual activation of a Vitae region cannot itself create a recognition event.**

# 16. MANA boundary

Vitae economy canon is explicit:

```text
You may pay for support.
You may not pay for recognition.
```

Therefore an icosahedral UI must not create:

- paid face completion;
- MANA acceleration between faces;
- premium shortcuts through the solid;
- purchasable glow or rank interpreted as becoming;
- rewards for completing all twenty regions;
- financial multipliers based on grade or specialization geometry.

Optional paid curriculum/media/tools may exist under existing economic constraints, but payment changes access to utility, never the semantic state of the jewel.

# 17. Geometry-free operation

The same Vitae registry must render without a solid.

Required equivalent representations include:

```text
Map
  Schools
    Grade I–X
  Specializations

Path
  current selected orientation
  actual dependencies / references

Record
  local practice sessions
  private notes

Authority
  separately recognized responsibility envelopes
```

This aligns directly with the existing Vitae structural UI audit.

The icosahedron should therefore be a **view of the registry**, not the registry itself.

Candidate law:

> **Registry Before Render — the Vitae corpus and dependency data must exist in a geometry-independent registry from which card, list, tree, and icosahedral views are derived.**

# 20. New candidate laws

## Corpus Count Does Not Grant Edge Meaning

Twenty named regions can justify twenty faces without justifying thirty relationships.

## Core Completion Is Global, Not Spoke-Based

Specialization eligibility cannot be inferred from whichever grade happens to be spatially adjacent.

## Elegant Decomposition Is Still a Hypothesis

A 10+5+5 factorization cannot create curricular families absent from doctrine.

## Opposite Pairing Requires Doctrine

Antipodal faces may only represent a duality already established independently.

## Do Not Complete the Solid by Count Rescue

Unassigned vertices may remain unassigned.

## Dependency Degree Is Data, Not Geometry

Curricular prerequisites may have any evidence-supported degree.

## Specialization Adjacency Is Not Exclusivity

Spatial closeness cannot silently define preferred specialization combinations.

## The Map Is Not the Journey

A corpus atlas may be fixed while the lived route remains participant-owned and contingent.

## Closed Surface, Open Journey

A complete geometric shell never means a completed human.

## No Geometric Progress Meter

Brightness, filled faces, area, volume, traversal percentage, or coverage may not become worth/readiness metrics.

## Coordinate Is Not Recognition

Selection or traversal is not a recognition event.

## Registry Before Render

Cards, lists, trees, and solids must render the same underlying geometry-independent Vitae data.

# 21. Decision

The audit **rejects the icosahedron as a fixed semantic dependency/progression graph for Vitae**.

It **provisionally accepts an icosahedral corpus-atlas hypothesis** because the current repository independently contains an exact, meaningful high-level twentyfold set:

```text
10 grades
+
10 specializations
=
20 primary curriculum regions
```

That is real architectural evidence.

The thirty face adjacencies have **not** earned semantic meaning.

The twelve vertices have **not** earned semantic identities.

The ten opposite-face pairs have **not** earned grade/specialization pairings.

Therefore the icosahedron is currently strongest as:

```text
jewel / atlas / terrain
```

not:

```text
scoreboard / dependency engine / recognition engine
```

The participant may move through the atlas.

Vitae recognition remains separate, stability-first, consent-respecting, and responsibility-shaped.
