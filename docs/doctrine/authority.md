# Authority (Vitae)

## Purpose

This document defines Vitae’s learning, recognition, and authority boundaries.

Vitae provides a voluntary ladder of learning, practice, and development, alongside a distinct process for recognizing growth demonstrated through lived experience.

Participants may pursue grades, specializations, or both, and may pause or leave the pathway. Progression describes learning and demonstrated competencies; it does not rank human worth.

Completing learning requirements may establish eligibility for recognition or responsibility. Recognition requires its applicable evidence and review. Architect and governance permissions require separate, explicit authorization; completion or payment alone does not grant them.

---

## Foundational Axiom

Becoming is sovereign.

No system, symbol, token, role, or authority may cause it.

**Recognition may only follow stabilization. Never precede it.**

When recognition is uncertain, silence is correct.

---

## What Vitae Is

Vitae combines a voluntary learning pathway with a distinct recognition layer of becoming.

It exists to:

- name maturation after it has occurred

- preserve continuity of growth across time

- enable responsibility to be entrusted without creating hierarchy of worth

Learning may support participant-chosen aspirations. Recognition must not become competitive ranking or a mechanism of coercion.

---

## What Vitae Is Not

Vitae is explicitly not:

- a hierarchy of human worth

- an automatic route from course completion to recognition, governance authority, or system permissions

- a reputation score

- a prestige system

- a proxy for value, merit, or identity

No being is more worthy because of a Vitae state.

---

## Authority & Layer Placement

Vitae authority is distributed across layers:

- **primary authority:** lived experience

- **recognition interface:** application (read-only)

- **optional anchoring:** protocol (witness only)

Vitae has:

- no economic authority

- no temporal authority

- no coercive power

---

## Responsibility Without Worth

Vitae is the primary gate for responsibility across Arcanum.

It may gate:

- stewardship

- guardianship

- custodial roles

- advanced responsibilities that affect others

Vitae may never gate:

- worth

- belonging

- dignity

- participation

Responsibility is entrusted. Worth is inherent.

---

## Permanence & Accretion

Vitae recognitions are permanent once recorded.

- recognitions do not decay

- recognitions are not revoked

- multiple recognitions for the same domain may coexist

Becoming is accretion, not replacement.

---

## Privacy & Visibility

Vitae recognitions are private by default.

Any public visibility must remain optional or strictly bounded to safety/coherence needs.

---

## System-Assisted Recognition (Bounded)

The system may:

- observe patterns

- identify potential recognitions

- offer reflective prompts

The system may not:

- declare becoming

- assign identity

- force recognition

Recognition affirms lived experience and self-identification.

---

## Interaction With Time (Tempus)

Vitae may reference elapsed cycles, never speed.

- time passing does not cause advancement

- participation does not guarantee recognition

- missing windows has no negative effect on Vitae

Time witnesses continuity. Vitae recognizes stabilization.

---

## Interaction With Economy (MANA)

Vitae recognition is economically sterile. Optional curriculum access and process support remain distinct from recognition and permission; payment does not guarantee either.

MANA may not:

- purchase recognition

- accelerate readiness

- unlock becoming

Vitae recognition states may never be sold, traded, staked, or priced.

---

## Forbidden Failure Modes

If any of the following appear, Vitae has been violated:

- min-max strategies

- best-path recommendations

- competitive ranking

- speed-based progression

- economic shortcuts

- simulated outcomes

These are doctrinal failures, not UX issues.

---

## Enforcement

Vitae authority is enforced through:

- doctrinal canon

- architectural constraints

- review by the Architect

When in doubt: do nothing.

False recognition is worse than silence.
